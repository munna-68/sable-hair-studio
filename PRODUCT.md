# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: prospective small-business owners (salon owners and peers) viewing this site as a portfolio showcase on a bright laptop, judging in seconds whether this quality of website would make their business look expensive and win bookings.

Secondary (demo stand-ins): design-conscious salon clients in Seattle wanting a personal cut or color plan and a calm booking path. They never transact for real; their flows exist to prove function.

Job to be done: prove that a high-quality, functional website makes a local studio look premium and bookable, without a sales deck.

## Product Purpose

Sable Hair Studio is a showcase build for a clear-eyed color and cut studio. It demonstrates editorial marketing surfaces (Studio, Services, Memberships, About, Visit) plus real working flows (booking desk, service matcher, bag, saved looks, search) so a potential client can feel the end state for their own business.

Success looks like a visitor thinking this feels expensive, calm, and functional, then asking for the same standard on their site.

## Brand Personality

Calm, restrained, confident. Quiet expense over loud luxury. Exacting about details, warm in tone, never showy. Voice is direct and practical, CTAs name the next commitment, microcopy reassures with specifics.

## Anti-references

What this should NOT look like or feel like:

- Generic SaaS blue and black (cobalt buttons, ink-black panels, porcelain-blue chrome). The old Chromatic Cut system is the anti-reference.
- Warm cream-and-bronze comfort, and muted pine-on-stone flatness. Both earlier passes are retired. This system stays bright white, deep green, and vivid on purpose.
- Banded tinted sections and sharp square buttons everywhere. Sections share one ground with hairlines, not color blocks; the footer owns the dark; actions go pill.
- Cheap template salon sites and AI slop: gradient text, glassmorphism cards, identical icon-plus-heading card grids, hero-metric stats, side-stripe accent borders, modal-first flows.
- Conventional luxury-salon cliché: gold on black marble, script fonts, heavy shadows, stock-staged glamour.
- Editorial-magazine costume on a salon brief: italic display serif plus mono labels plus ruled columns for its own sake.
- Anything that reads AI-made at a glance. Restraint must carry intent, not emptiness.

## Design Principles

1. Restraint signals expense. Fewer colors, fewer weights, more whitespace. Every element earns its place.
2. Clarity is luxurious. Service, timing, and availability read before decoration on every screen.
3. Show function, do not decorate. Booking accumulation, next-visit reveals, and practical confirmations carry the premium feel.
4. Cool over cozy, bright over muted. White ground, pine-black ink, and one vivid green beat clinical blue precision, warm cream comfort, and flat pine quietness alike.
5. Prove the business value. Every flow should feel like something a real studio could run on, not a mockup.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for text and interactive states. Visible focus rings preserved and retinted to the new accent. Reduced-motion preferences disable parallax, marquee, and reveal choreography. No color-only signals; selected and availability states pair color with label or shape change. Targets meet 24px minimum, primary actions larger.
